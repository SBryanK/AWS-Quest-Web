# Content and source coverage

Inventory before teaching: source ID, filename/title, edition/date, page/section count, extraction method, and visual inspection notes. Use stable source and lesson IDs. For every page/section, record explained, reference-only, administrative, duplicate, unread, or unresolved; include the reason. A page range is traceability, not proof every detail has been taught. Record meaningful omissions and do not hide them in an overall percentage.

A lesson should answer:
1. What is it, in familiar language?
2. What mechanism makes it work?
3. When does it fit and what constraint changes the choice?
4. What is a concrete worked example, including units/assumptions?
5. What is a plausible alternative and why does it fail here?
6. Which terms must the learner recognize?
7. Can the learner explain it without seeing the answer?

Retain English domain terms; explain relationships in the requested language. Do not translate proper service names into invented product names. Give acronyms their expansion, plain-language meaning, and a memory cue. For non-technical subjects, substitute theories, processes, or decision frameworks for services.

Separate source assertions, authored explanation, external corrections, and unresolved conflicts. Keep citations beside the relevant lesson/table. Verify niche/volatile claims with primary sources; never silently overwrite a historical fact with a current one. Record both when chronology matters.

Transform source material in original language and examples. For a public site, do not automatically redistribute a copyrighted full deck/book, private client files, answer keys, or learner notes. Keep references and, when needed, a local user-selected PDF viewer. Public hosting access and source/document permissions are distinct. Do not send files to third-party services without task authorization.
